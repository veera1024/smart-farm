Agro-Climatic Cycles

Andhra Pradesh Growing Seasons

Aligning seed sowing with the Southwest Monsoon, Northeast post-monsoon showers, and summer lift irrigation ensures steady yields and minimizes weather risk.

Seasonal Blueprints

The Three Cropping Seasons

Understand key sowing windows, water dependencies, and crop candidates for each season across Andhra Pradesh districts.

Season 01 • Monsoon

Kharif (Sarva)

June – July Sowing • September – December Harvest

The Kharif season accounts for over 65% of Andhra Pradesh's total cultivated area. Initiated by the arrival of the Southwest Monsoon in early June, it powers the rice bowls of Krishna and Godavari deltas as well as rainfed groundnut and cotton tracts in Rayalaseema.

Major Crops
Paddy, Cotton, Groundnut, Redgram, Maize, Castor.
Water Source
Southwest monsoon rainfall, Nagarjuna Sagar & Godavari canals.
Key Challenge
Dry spells during tillering/pegging; cyclone rains during harvest.
Season 02 • Post-Monsoon Winter

Rabi (Dalva)

October – December Sowing • February – April Harvest

Rabi benefits from Northeast Monsoon showers and stored reservoir water. Cooler temperatures and bright sunny days reduce pest incidence compared to Kharif, resulting in higher grain quality and excellent yields for pulses and maize.

Major Crops
Bengal Gram, Rabi Paddy, Rabi Maize, Blackgram, Sunflower.
Water Source
Conserved residual soil moisture, tube wells, reservoir canals.
Key Challenge
Terminal heat stress in March-April during grain filling.
Season 03 • Hot Catch Crop

Summer (Zaid)

January – March Sowing • April – June Harvest

A short, intensive window between Rabi and Kharif. Suitable only where farmers have reliable irrigation systems (drip or functional borewells). Offers rapid income through green fodder, pulses, and horticulture.

Major Crops
Watermelon, Greengram (Moong), Bhendi, Cucumber, Cowpea.
Water Source
Borewell with micro-irrigation or farm ponds.
Key Challenge
High evaporation rates; water table exhaustion.
